# Sample size or power must be justified before running the experiment, not after

Decide, before collecting any data, how many trials or samples are needed to
detect the effect size you actually care about (a power calculation, or at
minimum a stated minimum-n rule), rather than running until a result looks
good and rationalizing the sample size afterward. Post hoc justification is a
leading route to false positives: a small sample can produce a
dramatic-looking effect purely by chance, and a story written after seeing
the result cannot rule out that the sample size was chosen, consciously or
not, because it happened to produce a winning number. Sample-size or
power justification belongs in the pre-registration step, fixed before the
test runs, alongside the numeric decision threshold and the falsification
criterion, not retrofitted into the write-up once results are in.
